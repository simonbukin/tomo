# tomo marks — exploration

Open `comparison.html` in a browser. It is a contact sheet, not a proposal. It
answers one question: does the mark still feel intentional when tiny?

Each mark sits on a 16 unit grid, with every edge on a whole unit, so it stays
crisp at 16 px. The ink is `currentColor`; a monochrome context can override
the purple, `var(--brand-accent, #7d4dff)`. The app is not changed.

## A — abstract t, `tomo-mark-a.svg`

The mark the app has now, cut on a finer grid: a stem, a crossbar, a foot, and
one small accent square as a registration mark. Bars and counters are both 3 px
at 16 px, so the letter keeps its shape.
Trade-off: it is a letter, so it must compete with every other lowercase t. The
accent square is only 2 px at 16 px. It can read as dirt on the corner.

## B — folded tab, `tomo-mark-b.svg`

A squat body with a raised tab, in the proportion of an index divider. It has
the most mass of the three, so it is the calmest at 16 px.
Trade-off: mass is all it has. A body with a tab is also the shape of a macOS
folder. B is legible at 16 px, but legible as somebody else's icon.

## C — companion pieces, `tomo-mark-c.svg`

A block with a bite cut from one corner, and a second block that sits in the
bite with a 2 px gap. Two pieces, one keeps the other. No people, no handshake.
Trade-off: the whole idea is in a 2 px gap. At 16 px on the dark field the
light ink can bloom and close it, and then it is one lumpy square. The sheet
exists to settle this.

## Recommendation

A. It is the only candidate that is still a specific thing at 16 px, it is
already in the product, and it stays a t in monochrome. Then look hard at its
accent square: at 16 px it adds noise, not identity.

I could not look at the rendered page. The claims come from the geometry and
the pixel grid, not from my eye.
